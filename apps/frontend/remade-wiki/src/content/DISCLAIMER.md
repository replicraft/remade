## About Remade

Remade is an independent, community-maintained directory of software reimplementations. It provides informational listings and links to third-party projects. Remade does not host or distribute the implementations listed in its directory and does not develop or maintain them unless expressly stated.

## Independent Development

Remade is intended to catalog independently developed reimplementations. Projects submitted for inclusion are expected not to incorporate unauthorized source code or copyrighted assets from the software they seek to reimplement.

Clean-room development is a recognized approach to implementing compatible software while reducing the risk of copying protected expression. A claim of clean-room development is made by a project's authors, not certified by Remade. Remade does not independently audit development practices or guarantee that any listed project follows a particular methodology.

## Legal Precedent

Independent software reimplementation and interoperability have been examined in legal decisions, including *Sega Enterprises Ltd. v. Accolade, Inc.*, 977 F.2d 1510 (9th Cir. 1992)<sup><a href="#source-1">[1]</a></sup>, and *Sony Computer Entertainment, Inc. v. Connectix Corp.*, 203 F.3d 596 (9th Cir. 2000)<sup><a href="#source-2">[2]</a></sup>. Those decisions addressed particular reverse-engineering practices and fair-use questions under U.S. law; they do not establish blanket legality for all reimplementations.

A notable historical example is **Phoenix Technologies' IBM PC-compatible BIOS**, released in 1984. Phoenix used a documented separation between analysis/specification and independently written implementation to produce a compatible BIOS.<sup><a href="#source-3">[3]</a></sup> This is an example of clean-room engineering practice, **not a court judgment declaring every such reimplementation lawful**.

In *Google LLC v. Oracle America, Inc.*, 593 U.S. 1, 141 S. Ct. 1183 (April 5, 2021), the U.S. Supreme Court held that Google's use of portions of the Java API to build Android constituted fair use.<sup><a href="#source-4">[4]</a></sup>

These historical examples illustrate approaches to interoperability, but the legal status of any project depends on its specific facts and applicable law. Inclusion in Remade is not a legal opinion or certification of compliance.

## Account Data

Signing in shares your provider account ID, username, optional display name and avatar, and verified email addresses when available. Remade uses verified email to help detect duplicate accounts. Raw email addresses are not saved; private email fingerprints are retained for matching and are not published. Fingerprints are not anonymous data. Session tokens are stored as hashes, and request votes and builder indications are associated with your Remade account. Signing in is optional for browsing the directory.

Connecting another sign-in method requires you to be signed in to Remade and authorize that provider. Matching names or email addresses do not automatically connect accounts.
